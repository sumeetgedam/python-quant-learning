# Lesson 5: Correlation

## Learning objectives

- Explain what correlation measures.
- Interpret the sign and strength of correlation.
- Understand why correlation does not prove causation.

## Core concepts

**Correlation** measures how two variables move in relation to each other.

The **Pearson correlation coefficient**, usually written as `r`, measures the direction and strength of a linear relationship. Its value ranges from `-1` to `+1`.

- `r = +1` : perfect positive linear relationship.
- `r = 0`  : no linear relationship.
- `r = -1` : perfect negative linear relationship.

A positive correlation means higher values of one variable tend occur with higher values of the other. A negative correlation means higher values of one tend to occur with lower values of the other.

A correlation near zero does not rule out a **nonlinear** relationship.

## Correlation is not causation

If two variables are correlated, that alone does not show that one causes the other.
They may be influenced by a third variable, or their relationship may be coincidental.

**Example:** Ice-cream sales and swimming activity may both increase in warm weather. That correlation does not mean ice-cream sales causes swimming.

## Example

Suppose two stocks have a correlation of `0.8` over a selected period. Their returns tended to move together in a positive linear pattern during that period. This does not guarantee that they will continue to do so, or explain why they moved together.

## Interview questions

1. What does a correlation coefficient of `-0.7` indicate ?
2. Does a correlation of zero mean there is no relationship of any kind ?
3. Why does correlation not prove causation ?
4. Can correlation change depending on the time period or data used ?

## Exercise

A dataset has a Pearson correlation of `-0.9` between two variables.

1. Is the relationship positive or negative ?
2. Is it weak or strong as a linear relationship ?
3. Does this result prove that one variable causes the other ?

## Recap

- Correlation describes the direction and strength of association
- Pearson correlation measures linear association and ranges from `-1` to `+1`.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
- A near-zero Pearson correlation can still occur when a nonlinear relationship exists.
