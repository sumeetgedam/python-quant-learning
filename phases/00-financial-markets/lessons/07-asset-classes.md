# Lesson 7: Asset Classes

## Learning objectives

- Name common financial asset classes.
- Understand how asset classes differ.
- Distinguish an asset class from a financial instrument.

## Core concepts

An **asset class** is a broad group of investments with similar characteristics.

### Equities

Equities represent ownership in companies. Their prices can rise or fall, and some companies pay dividends.

### Fixed Income

Fixed income instruments, such as bonds, generally involve lending money to an issuer in return for scheduled interest and repayment terms. Payments and repayment are subject to the issuer's ability to meet its obligations.

### Foreign exchange (FX)

FX is the market for exchanging currencies, such as euros for U.S. dollars. Currency prices are quoted as pairs.

### Commodities

Commodities are basic goods such as oil, natural gas, gold, or agricultural products. Investors can gain exposure through different instruments, including futures and funds.

### Cash and cash equivalents

These include cash and short-term instruments that are relatively liquid. They are often use for transactions, reserves, or short-term investment.

### Derivatives

A **derivative** is a contract whose value depends on an underlying asset, rate, index, or other reference. Options and Futures are examples. Derivatives are financial instruments, but they are not always treated as separate asset class.

## Why tis matters for analytics

Different asset classes have different market conventions, data formats, trading hours, and risk drivers. Before analyzing a dataset, identify what the instrument represents and how its prices and returns are defined.

## Example

A portfolio contains company shares, government bonds, and gold futures. It has exposure to equities, fixed income, and commodities. The futures contract are derivatives whose value depends on the price of gold.
