# Lesson 6: ETF and Index Basics

## Learning objectives

- Explain what a market index measures.
- Distinguish an index from an ETF
- Understand how an ETF can track an index.

## Core concepts

### Market index

An **index** measures the performance of a selected group of securities according to defined rules.

For example, an equity index might track companies selected by market size or another criterion. The index is a calculation not a fund that investors directly buy.

### Exchange-traded fund (ETF)

An **ETF** is an investment fund whose shares trade on an exchange. An ETF may hold a basket of securities and can be designed to track an index.

Investors buy and sell ETF shares in the market, much like stocks. The ETF's market price can differ slightly from the value of its underlying holdings.

### Trackign an index

An index ETF may hold all the securities in the index or use a sampling approach. Its return may differ from the index's return because of feeds. trading costs, cash holdings, and tracking differences.

### ETF versus individual stock

- An individual stock represents ownership in one company.
- An ETF share represents an interest in a fund that may hold many investments.
- An index is a benchmark calculation; it is not itself an ETF.

## Example

Suppose an index tracks 100 companies. An ETF designed to follow that index holds shares in those companies, or a representative sample of them. Buying the ETF gives an investor exposure to the fund's holdings rather than direct ownership of every company in the index.

## Interview questions

1. What is the difference between an index and an ETF ?
2. Can an investor directly buy an index ?
3. Why might an ETF's return differ from the index it tracks ?
4. How does holding an ETF differ from holding one company's stock ?

## Exercise

An ETF is designed to track an index of 50 companies.

1. Is the index itself a fund ?
2. What does an investor own when buying a share of the ETF ?
3. Name one reason the ETF's return might differ from the index's return.

## Recap

- An index measure the performance of a defined group of securities.
- An ETF is a fund whose shares trade on an exchange.
- An index ETF aims to track an index, but its return may differ slightly.
