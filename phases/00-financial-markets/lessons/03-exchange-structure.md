# Lesson: Exchange Structure

## Learning objectives

- Explain what an exchange does.
- Understand bids, asks, and the bid-ask spread.
- Recognize that stocks can trade on multiple venues.

## Core concepts

### What is an exchange ?

An example is a marketplace where buyers and sellers can submit orders to trade financial instruments. It provides rules and systems for displaying and matching orders.

An exchange is **not necessarily the only place** where a stock can trade. Depending on the market, trades can also occur on other trading venues, such as Alternative Trading Systems.

### Order book

An **order book** records resting buy and sell orders for an instrument.

- **Bid:** the highest displayed price a buyer is currently willing to pay.
- **Ask** (or offer): the lowest displayed price a seller is currently willing to accept.
- **Bid size / ask size:** the quantity available to those prices.


### Bid-ask spread

The **bid-ask spread** is the difference between the best ask and the best bid.

For example, if the best bid is $49.98 and the best ask is $50.02, the spread is $0.04.

A smaller spread often indicates that buyers' and sellers' displayed prices are closer together. The spread can change as oredrs arrive, change, or are canceled.

### Matching orderss

A trading venue applies its matching rules to determine when orders can trade. For example, a marketable buy order may match with available sell orders, starting at the lowest available ask.

Exact venue rules and order behavior can vary.

## Example

The order book shows : 

| Side | Price  | Quantity |
|------|--------|----------|
| Ask  | $50.02 | 100      |
| Bid  | $49.98 | 200      |

The best bid is $49.98; the best ask is $50.02; the spread is $0.04.

A buyer willing to pay $50.02 could potentially trade with the seller offering shares at that price, subject to order priority and available quantity.

## Recap

- Exchanges provide organized systems and rules for trading.
- The order book displays resting buy and sell orders.
- The spread is the best ask minus the best bid.
- A stock may trade across multiple venues.
