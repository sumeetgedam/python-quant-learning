# Lesson 5: Corporate Actions

## Learning objectives

- Explain what a corporate action is.
- Recognize common corporate actions.
- Understand why they matter when analyzing historical data.

## Core concepts

A **corporate action** is an event initiated by a company that can affect its securities or shareholders.

### Dividends

A company may distribute some of its earnings or cash to shareholders. A cash dividend is paid per eligible share. Dividends are not guaranteed.

### Stock splits

A stock split increases the number of shares while proportionally reducing the price per share.  In a **20-for-1 split**, each shareholder receive two shares for every one share held.

The split changes the number of shares and quoted price, but does not by itself change the total value of a shareholder's holding.

### Mergers and acquisitions

When companies merge or one company acquires another, shareholders may receive cash, shares in another company, or a combination, depending on the transaction.

### Rights Issues

A company may give existing shareholders the right to buy additional shares, often at a specified price, under stated terms.

## Why corporate actions matter for data analysis

Corporate actions can create apparent jumps or changes in historical prices. When analyzing returns, price series may need to be adjusted so that events such as splits and dividends are handled consistently.

Always check what a dataset's prices represent; for example, whether they are raw or adjusted prices.

## Example : 2-for-1 split

Before the split, an investor holds 10 shares priced at $100 each: a total value of $1,000.

After a 2-for-1 split, the investor holds 20 shares. If the price adjusts to $50 pr share, the total remains $1,000, ignoring market movements and other factors.

## Interview questions

1. What is a corporate action ?
2. What changes for a shareholder in a 2-for-1 stock split ?
3. Why might a stock split create a misleading jump in an unadjusted price chart ?
4. What is the difference between a raw price and an adjusted price ?


## Exercise

A company announces a 3-for-1 stock split. An investor owns 6 shares before the split.

1. How may shares will the investor hold afterward ?
2. If the pre-split price was $80, what would the proportional post-split price be ?
3. What is the holding's value before and after, ignoring market movements ?

## Recap

- Corporate actions are company events that affect securities or shareholders.
- Splits change share count and per-share price proportionally.
- Dividends and other events can affect shareholder value and historical data.
- Check how corporate actions are represented before analyzing price series.
